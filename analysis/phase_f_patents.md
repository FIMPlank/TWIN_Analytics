# Phase F: predetermined digital-patent stock vs regional industrial growth (and ETS)

Status: revised after independent review (Romania population fix, effective-cluster diagnostics, selection disclosure, MDE). Local files only, nothing committed.
Reproduce: `python scripts/download_eurostat_patents.py; python scripts/download_eurostat_regional_gva.py; python analysis/phase_f_patents.py > analysis/output/phase_f_log.txt` (about 6 min; fixed seeds).
Code: `analysis/phase_f_patents.py` (regressions), `phase_f_build.py` / `phase_f_build_units.py` (data build, NUTS harmonisation). Full printed output: `analysis/output/phase_f_log.txt`. Tables: `analysis/output/phase_f_results.csv` (every regression), `phase_f_leave_one_out.csv`, `phase_f_qc_*.csv`, `phase_f_ets_country*.csv`, `phase_f_country_growth.csv`. Figure: `analysis/output/phase_f_headline.png`.

## 1. Headline

**No evidence of a large effect of predetermined digital patent capability on regional industrial growth.** With country fixed effects, a digital-patent stock fixed by 2004 does not predict regional industrial GVA growth 2005-2019, at NUTS2 or NUTS3, in EU-15 or in accession countries. The claim-regression coefficient is -0.05 (NUTS2, n=183) and -0.03 (NUTS3, n=995) pp/yr per unit of asinh(stock per million inhabitants), 95% CIs about [-0.30, +0.19] and [-0.17, +0.12]. The positive Phase D correlation shows up only *between* countries (pooled, no FE), where it is marginal and shrinks to insignificance once non-digital patenting and an accession dummy are added.

This is a bound, not a "no effect" finding. The minimum detectable effect (80% power, 5% two-sided, t(G-1)) for the claim coefficient is 0.34 (NUTS2) and 0.20 (NUTS3) pp/yr per unit, i.e. about 0.37 and 0.27 pp/yr per within-country SD of the digital regressor (the Reviewer's independent figure is 0.23-0.29 per SD). Only large effects are excluded. A causal effect of digitalization is neither shown nor ruled out.

**Effective clusters.** The nominal G is 19 (NUTS2) and 25 (NUTS3), but the claim CI at NUTS3 is effectively a two-country object: effective number of clusters G* = 2.0 (score-variance version; 5.1 by leverage), with **Italy contributing 69% of the score variance**. At NUTS2 G* = 5.6 (top: France, 29%). See section 4.

## 2. Data and construction (choices documented)

- Patents: `pat_ep_rtot` / `pat_ep_ripc` (EPO applications by priority year, NUTS3, 1977-2012), unit NR. Assignment is by inventor residence with fractional counting across regions (values like 40.7) and across IPC classes: sections A-H sum to the IPC total (59,024 vs 59,037 in 2004), so digital + non-digital = total and there is no double counting. I state the inventor-residence attribution from Eurostat's documentation as I know it; I could not fetch the ESMS page, so treat it as unverified against the live metadata.
- Digital patents (core): IPC classes G06 (computing), G11 (information storage), H03 (basic electronic circuitry), H04 (electric communication). This is the class-level core of the OECD/Eurostat ICT definition; the dataset only offers 3-character classes, so subclass-based OECD ICT definitions (e.g. G01S, H01L, H04N-level detail) are not reproducible. Broad variant (robustness): core plus H01, G05, G08, G09. Digital core is 13.9% of EPO applications in 2004 (7.4% in 1990), which means the digital share rose steadily and the stock is dominated by post-1990 patents.
- Non-digital comparison: total minus digital core (main); mechanical/chemical sections B+C+F (robustness).
- Stock: perpetual inventory S_t = (1-d)S_{t-1} + P_t from 1977, ending 2004 (d = 15%; d = 10%, 20% and a plain cumulative count as robustness). Pre-1977 patents are ignored (negligible at 15%).
- Normalisation: per million inhabitants in 2004 (`nama_10r_3popgdp`), then asinh (zeros: 5.9% of NUTS2 and 16.6% of NUTS3 units have zero digital stock). Robustness: per employed person, per unit of GVA, log1p.
- Outcome: 100 x [ln(B-E GVA 2019) - ln(B-E GVA 2005)] / 14, current prices in national currency (`nama_10r_3gva`). Regional volume series (PYP) exist for only 9 countries, so I do not use them. With country FE the national deflator is absorbed exactly; the no-FE specs subtract the national B-E deflator. Window ends in 2019 to avoid COVID. NUTS2 = sum of NUTS3 children (NaN if any child is missing).
- Controls: ln(total GVA per capita 2004, EUR), industrial (B-E) share of GVA 2004.
- Sample: EU27 only (CH, TR, NO dropped; UK has patents but no regional GVA in these tables, so all 139 UK NUTS3 are lost).
- **Romania population fix.** `nama_10r_3popgdp` has no Romanian NUTS3 population for 2002-2011 (values only for 2000-01 and 2012+), which made `pop04` NaN and silently removed Romania from every per-capita regression in the first draft. Fallback (`fill_missing_regional_pop`; only Romania is affected): interpolate each region's share of national population linearly between its 2001 and 2012 shares and multiply by the national population of the year (Eurostat has national RO for all years). The 42 regional 2004 values sum exactly to the national 2004 figure. This touches only the normaliser (stock per inhabitant, GVA-per-capita control) and the boundary-stability population check, not patent counts or the outcome. It is an interpolation assumption; note that dropping RO changes the accession-only NUTS3 coefficient from -0.10 to -0.03.

## 3. QC of the patent panel and the NUTS-vintage merge (before any regression)

- Patents sit on **NUTS 2010** codes (DE has 412 NUTS3, MV has 18 districts); GVA, population and employment sit on **NUTS 2021**. A naive merge matches only 914 of 1,426 NUTS3 codes, and silently mis-assigns some.
- Harmonisation: Eurostat correspondence tables 2010->2013 and 2013->2016 (downloaded to `data/raw/geo/`); only pure recodes are followed, and any NUTS3 that was merged, split or boundary-shifted is flagged and dropped. The 2016->2021 table could not be downloaded (404), so that step is checked only by code-set comparison plus a population test.
- Independent boundary check: Eurostat's own patent-side population (NR / per-million) vs 2021-vintage population. Across 922 matched NUTS3, the median ratio is 1.000, 91% are within 2% and 99% within 5%. Units off by more than 5% are dropped (10 NUTS3). This does not prove identical boundaries, so residual risk remains for code reuse with a small boundary change.
- NUTS3 kept: 1,050 of 1,370 patent regions (EU27+CH+NO+TR+UK). Analysis sample **995 units, 25 countries** (1,050 NUTS3 kept by the merge, less non-EU27 units CH/NO/TR, units with a missing control or outcome, and CY and LU, which are single-unit and drop out of FE). The first draft's 956 was wrongly attributed to CY and LU; the largest unlisted loss was Romania's 39 units. By country, share of 1977-2004 patents retained: near 100% for AT, CZ, DE, DK, ES, FR, HU, IT, RO, SK and others; **EL 33%, HR 16%, PT 10%, SI 38%, FI 61%, NL 66%, IE 68%, LV 0%**. Overall 95.6% of EU27 patents (95.0% of digital).
- NUTS2 "strict" (child NUTS3 sets identical after mapping, no flags): 197 of 318 patent-side NUTS2 codes; analysis sample 183 units, 19 countries (EU15 12, accession 7: BG, CZ, HU, LT, PL, RO, SK). EE, HR, IE, LV, SI have no unit. Retained patents: 90.6% of all, 81.1% of digital, with NL only 5% of its digital patents (2010->2021 recoding). A "lenient" NUTS2 variant (tolerates NUTS3 reorganisation inside a NUTS2 if NUTS2 population matches within 5%) gives 200 units, 17 countries and is reported alongside.
- Patent-side facts: 24.6% of NUTS3-year cells are zero; unallocated regions (ZZ/XX) hold under 1% of patents; 8 cells have digital > total (clipped). EPO priority-year counts are truncated in 2011-2012 (49.8k in 2012 vs 59k in 2004); this affects only the 2005-2012 placebo stock, not the baseline.
- Raw corr(asinh digital, asinh non-digital) is 0.84-0.90, but that is mostly between-country. The Reviewer verified that the within-country correlation is 0.59-0.66 and that digital keeps 52-62% of its within-country variance after partialling out non-digital, so the digital-vs-general comparison is not a collinearity artefact.

### Selection: lost regions are not random

Regions are lost where NUTS codes were reorganised between 2010 and 2021 (concentrated in EL, PT, NL, HR, PL, FI, SI, IE, LV, parts of DE) and for the UK. Comparing all EU27 NUTS3 regions with an industrial growth figure (`phase_f_qc_selection.csv`):

- NUTS3 stage: kept 2.59 pp/yr (n=997) vs lost 2.60 (n=172), essentially equal on average after the Romania fix. The Reviewer measured 3.60 (lost) vs 2.37 (kept) on the first draft; that gap came from Romania (fast-growing accession economy) being wrongly counted as lost and is gone. Within groups, lost regions grew slower than kept ones (EU-15: 1.37 vs 1.89; accession: 5.03 vs 5.72).
- NUTS2 strict stage: kept 2.48 (n=888 NUTS3 children) vs lost 2.94 (n=281): the NUTS2 loss tilts toward faster-growing regions, and within groups the gap reverses (EU-15 lost 1.56 vs kept 1.88; accession 5.37 vs 5.69), so it is composition: accession economies (HR, SI, PL, LV, EE) are over-represented among the lost.
- Consequence: the retained accession subsample is a non-random subset of accession regions (BG, CZ, HU, LT, PL, RO, SK; the Baltic/Balkan/Slovenian regions with recoded NUTS are mostly out). If digital capability mattered more in the missing fast-growing regions, the accession split understates it; if not, it is unbiased. The bias cannot be signed from these data, so the accession-split null applies to the retained regions only.

## 4. Results (inference: country-clustered CR1; t(G-1); wild cluster bootstrap-t, restricted, Rademacher, 9,999 draws for claim rows, full enumeration if G<=12; Webb weights agree within about 0.02; naive z in the CSV/log only)

Claim-carrying regression (A3): industrial growth on digital stock, non-digital stock, controls, country FE.

| Sample | n | G | G* (score / leverage) | coef | se | 95% CI (t) | p t(G-1) | p wild | MDE 80% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NUTS2 strict | 183 | 19 | 5.6 / 9.5 (FR 29%) | -0.052 | 0.116 | -0.30, +0.19 | 0.66 | 0.67 | 0.34 |
| NUTS3 | 995 | 25 | **2.0** / 5.1 (**IT 69%**) | -0.025 | 0.068 | -0.17, +0.12 | 0.72 | 0.84 | 0.20 |
| NUTS2 lenient | 200 | 17 | 4.2 / 9.8 (FI 41%) | -0.037 | 0.119 | -0.30, +0.22 | 0.76 | 0.78 | 0.35 |

G* = 1/sum(w_g^2), with w_g the cluster's share of the target regressor's score variance (leverage version: share of residualised x'x). It is a simple score-based diagnostic in the spirit of Carter, Schnepel and Steigerwald (2017), not their exact estimator; the Reviewer's exact computation gives 1.9 (claim) and 3.0 (A4) at NUTS3, matching. The NUTS3 claim CI is effectively a statement about Italy vs the rest: dropping IT moves the NUTS3 A3 coefficient to about +0.05 (wild p 0.29). The nominal 25 clusters overstate the information, so t(G-1) intervals are optimistic. NUTS2 (G* about 5) is less concentrated but far below 19.

Other specifications, identical samples (full list in the log; every row prints G*):

- Digital only, with controls (A2): NUTS2 -0.018 (wild p 0.84); NUTS3 +0.068 (p t 0.035, wild p 0.14, Webb 0.12; G* 4.3). The NUTS3 t-based p is below 0.05 but does not survive the wild bootstrap, and disappears when non-digital patenting is added (A3), under equal country weights (+0.005, p 0.97) and when Germany is dropped. I do not count it.
- **Digital vs general patenting.** Non-digital coefficient in A3: +0.11 (NUTS2) and +0.27 (NUTS3), insignificant. Total patents alone (A4), NUTS3: +0.24, p t 0.072, wild p 0.011 (Webb 0.015; wild p<0.05 in 25/25 leave-one-out runs). **The wild p should not be read as evidence.** G* is 3.0 for this regression (Italy 45% of score variance), and the Reviewer found that the wild-bootstrap |t| distribution is narrower than t(23) (95th percentile of |t*| about 1.67 vs 2.07), i.e. the bootstrap is anti-conservative here. I had confirmed my implementation against an independent loop, but that shows the code is right, not that the bootstrap behaves with such concentrated clusters. My earlier framing that Germany's 40% share of *units* drove this was wrong: unit share is not the relevant quantity, score variance is, and the dominant cluster is Italy. The t(G-1) p (0.072) and the equal-weight version (t 0.059, wild 0.078) are the more credible readings: suggestive at most. At NUTS2, A4 is +0.05 (p 0.77, G* 2.6), lenient +0.19 (p 0.40). The tilt spec (A5, digital minus non-digital) is -0.08 (NUTS2) and -0.15 (NUTS3), both insignificant: no evidence that digital-heavy specialisation beats general patenting.
- Pooled, no country FE (B), growth minus national deflator: digital + controls +0.32 (NUTS2; t p 0.022, wild 0.074) and +0.33 (NUTS3; t p 0.002, wild 0.051; G* 2.4, Greece 63%). Adding non-digital: +0.17 / +0.09 (wild p 0.16 / 0.17). Adding an accession dummy: +0.11 / +0.03 (wild 0.20 / 0.60). So the between-country association is marginal under wild inference and absorbed by general patenting and the accession split. It mirrors Phase D with a predetermined regressor, so reverse causality from later growth cannot explain it; a shared long-run driver can.
- Accession split of the claim regression: EU-15 -0.011 (NUTS2, G=12), -0.031 (NUTS3, G=13, G* 1.9, Italy 71%); accession -0.30 (NUTS2, G=7, CI [-1.03, +0.43], wild p 0.64) and -0.097 (NUTS3, G=12, wild p 0.29, MDE about 0.29). The dig x accession interaction is -0.28 (p 0.34) and -0.16 (p 0.15). No positive accession effect and no evidence of a 2.4x larger effect. Leave-one-out within the NUTS3 accession group: -0.03 (drop RO) to -0.17 (drop HU), no wild p below 0.16. See the selection caveat in section 3.
- Robustness (digital coefficient, A3 sample): depreciation 10/20% and cumulative, per-employment and per-GVA normalisation, log1p, broad digital definition, mech/chem comparator, population control, endpoint-averaged outcome, manufacturing outcome, growth 2005-12 and 2012-19 separately: NUTS2 all wild p at least 0.20 and NUTS3 at least 0.31; all |coef| below 0.24. Total-economy growth at NUTS3 is +0.044 (t p 0.055, wild 0.15): no digital specificity to industry.
- **Leave-one-country-out on the claim regression (A3):** NUTS2 coefficient range [-0.115, +0.019], no run with p<0.05; NUTS3 [-0.057, +0.044], none; lenient [-0.088, +0.046], none. For NUTS3 A2, LOO t p<0.05 in 22/25 runs and wild p<0.05 in 1/25; for NUTS3 A4, LOO wild p<0.05 in 25/25 (subject to the bootstrap concern above) and t p<0.05 in 1/25. Tables in `phase_f_leave_one_out.csv`.

## 5. Falsification / placebo (data reach back only to 2000, so window 2000-2004, 4 years, is short and noisy)

Regional GVA starts in 2000, so a true 1995-2004 placebo is **infeasible**. What I could run (NUTS2 n=174, G=18; NUTS3 n=959, G=24; stock from 2005-12 patents only, delta 15%; controls at 2000):

- P0 (stock through 1999, predetermined for 2000-04 growth): NUTS2 -0.25 (wild p 0.17), NUTS3 -0.10 (p 0.15). Not positive: the earlier-window replication of the design does not reproduce a positive sign.
- P1 (stock built only from 2005-2012 patents, growth 2000-04, the placebo): NUTS2 -0.09 (p 0.70), NUTS3 -0.13 (p 0.27). Passes, but weakly informative: the placebo stock correlates 0.83-0.89 (digital) and 0.93-0.96 (non-digital) with the earlier stock, so a pass is expected under no effect and is not strong evidence either way.
- P4 (total-economy growth 2000-04 on 1999 stock): NUTS2 -0.15 (t p 0.081, wild 0.072), lenient -0.13 (wild 0.060), NUTS3 +0.03 (p 0.76). With Romania included, the earlier NUTS2 "significant" negative is no longer below 0.05; its sign is opposite to the hypothesis anyway.

## 6. Country-level descriptives (N about 24-27; no cluster correction possible, so HC3 with t(N-k) and LOO ranges; extreme caution)

- National industrial real growth 2005-2019 (CLV20) on country stock (N=27): digital alone -0.26 (p 0.066), with controls -0.52 (p 0.064), + non-digital -0.27 (p 0.64), + accession dummy -0.09 (p 0.88). Sign is negative (the opposite of Phase D's correlation), indicating that country-level associations are fragile to specification and that the +0.073 relation is not reproduced with a predetermined stock at country level.
- Verified EU-ETS (`20-99`, GR->EL, GB->UK) 100 x ln(E_end/E_2005): N=24 (BG, RO, HR have no 2005 ETS data; UK dropped because no population in my regional source), end 2019 and 2023. Spearman with digital stock per capita +0.05 / +0.04; OLS digital alone +0.9 (p 0.75) and +0.85 (p 0.80); with non-digital +13.7 (p 0.21) and +10.6 (p 0.39); adding GDP per capita and accession dummy +13.7 (p 0.25), +11.8 (p 0.42). Nothing significant; the sign, if anything, is that more digital-patent-heavy countries cut emissions *less*, driven by near-collinearity (digital vs non-digital). ETS scope changed in 2013 and 2021, so levels are not like-for-like. The emissions null is unchanged.

## 7. What this does and does not show

- It shows: no evidence of a large within-country association between a stock of digital patents fixed by 2004 and subsequent regional industrial growth (bounded by the MDE above); a between-country association that is at best marginal and is absorbed by general patenting and the accession split. This is consistent with the Phase D story of a shared convergence driver.
- It does not show that digitalization has no effect (MDE 0.2-0.35 pp/yr per unit; smaller effects are compatible with the data). A patent stock measures inventive capacity located where inventors live, not digital adoption by firms in the region (adoption diffusion from elsewhere is invisible). Digital patents are concentrated in a few metropolitan/innovation hubs (CI width reflects that), and inventor location differs from where industrial value added is produced.
- A predetermined stock rules out reverse causality from later growth, but not shared long-run drivers: rich, educated, innovative regions have both patents and growth potential, and controls (baseline GDP per capita, industry share, non-digital stock, country FE) are coarse. The design cannot separate them.
- Power is limited: effective clusters are about 2 (NUTS3 claim) to 6 (NUTS2), 7 nominal clusters for accession NUTS2. A true effect of +0.2 pp/yr per unit could not be excluded.
- The regional GVA is nominal (national currency) and endpoint-based; it includes price effects that differ by region within country (e.g. commodity-heavy regions) and is noisy for small NUTS3 units.

## 8. Caveats (top items for the reviewer)

1. **Effective clusters are far fewer than nominal**: G* about 2.0 (NUTS3 claim, Italy 69%) and 3.0 (NUTS3 total patents), about 5.6 at NUTS2. NUTS3 p-values are optimistic; the total-patents wild p=0.011 is anti-conservative (bootstrap |t| narrower than t(23)) and is not evidence.
2. **Selection**: NUTS-vintage losses (EL, PT, HR, SI, NL, FI, IE, LV, PL, parts of DE; UK entirely) are not random; the NUTS2 loss tilts toward faster-growing accession regions, so the accession split covers a retained subset with unknown bias direction. See `phase_f_qc_coverage.csv` and `phase_f_qc_selection.csv`.
3. **NUTS harmonisation is partial** (2016->2021 not table-verified, population check only) and Romanian population for 2002-11 is interpolated (disclosed above); the first draft had silently dropped Romania.
4. The claim regression (A3) was fixed before running; about 60 specifications are listed, so single p-values just under 0.05 (NUTS3 A2, t-based) should be discounted.
5. Placebo is short (4 years) and the future stock is highly correlated with the baseline; 2011-12 patents are truncated.
6. Deviations: 1995-2004 falsification infeasible; UK absent from outcomes; class-level proxy for the OECD ICT definition; inventor-residence attribution from documentation, not re-verified live. Patent counting is fractional across IPC classes (the earlier download-script docstring said otherwise and has been corrected; sections A-H sum to the total).
